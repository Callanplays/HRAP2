# How live chemistry was fixed

Live chemistry predicts which gases form and their temperature at a specified chamber pressure, conserving the atoms and inlet enthalpy. The default HRAP simulation still uses its stored MATLAB-derived tables; those data and the standard engine were not changed.

## What broke, and what changed

1. **An empty charge equation stopped every default solve.** The old code included electrons in its balance equations but selected only neutral products. The new solver explicitly models neutral gases and builds equations only for the elements in the feed. Requests for charged reactants or products are rejected instead of partially modeled.
2. **The short product list made some mixtures impossible.** Rich mixtures need more possible carbon-bearing molecules. Sulfur and aluminum feeds also need products containing those elements. Products now come from every compatible neutral gas in the bundled database. The solver checks that every input element has an available output.
3. **The old hand-written iteration was fragile.** It has been replaced by a bounded SciPy least-squares solve of element-potential equations. Species amounts stay positive through logarithms. Temperature stays within the shared range of the selected thermodynamic data. Dependent balance equations are allowed; the final conservation checks determine whether the result is usable.
4. **A stop flag is no longer enough.** The solver recomputes each equation after stopping. Atom balances, mole-fraction normalization and scaled enthalpy residuals must each be below `1e-8`; total mass must agree within `1e-6`. Temperature, molecular mass and heat capacities must be finite and physical. Unconverged results retain a reason and are rejected by the table builder.
5. **Molecular masses disagreed with fuel formulas.** Built-in recipe masses are now calculated from the database's atomic masses. For example, the listed ABS formula implies 118.15586 kg/kmol, not 119.16; the metalized-plastisol recipe implies 97.07138, not 86. These changes make the existing formulas internally consistent; they do not establish that those formulas represent a particular manufactured fuel.
6. **Returned properties were silently clipped.** That clipping is removed. Accepted temperatures, molecular masses and heat-capacity ratios are returned unchanged. Specific heat and mean molecular mass use the actual product mass, rather than assuming the molar amount always represents exactly 1 kg.

The earlier repair that removed fixed-value fallback results remains in place. A failed solve cannot become a table of substitute properties.

## What was checked

- All eight bundled fuels generate their 6-by-8 live tables: 384 pressure/mixture points.
- Twelve saved reference cases cover all eight fuels, rich and lean mixtures, and several pressures. They were generated by Cantera's separate Gibbs-minimization solver, initialized independently of HRAP's answer. Tests compare temperature, molecular mass, frozen heat capacity, gamma and composition.
- Both solvers use identical NASA9 coefficients, neutral gas products, database atomic masses, inlet conditions and 100,000 Pa reference pressure. Cantera's universal gas constant differs slightly from HRAP's inherited 8314 J/(kmol K); the generator explicitly accounts for this when comparing enthalpy and specific heat.
- Regression tests include a pure-steam case with redundant element balances, the old infeasible product shortlist, insufficient iterations, missing-element products, unsupported ions, inconsistent reactant mass and preservation of unclipped output.
- Verification on September 22, 2026: all 128 repository tests passed; Pyright reported no errors or warnings. The native macOS app completed both live-chemistry and stored-table runs and displayed the unsupported-oxygen error while restoring its controls.

Reference data: [`tests/fixtures/chemistry_cantera.json`](../tests/fixtures/chemistry_cantera.json). Regenerate it from the repository root with `python scripts/make_chemistry_reference.py` after installing `cantera==3.2.0`. Cantera is a verification dependency, not a dependency of the app or normal tests. The file records the thermodynamic-data hash and Cantera version.

## Model limits and inlet assumptions

The app's live chemistry currently supports nitrous oxide only. Selecting Oxygen with live chemistry reports an error instead of combining oxygen tank properties with nitrous combustion properties.

This is **neutral ideal-gas equilibrium**, not a complete combustion or multiphase model. It does not include ionization, solid carbon/soot, condensed alumina, finite-rate chemistry or heat loss. In particular, successful sulfur/aluminum gas calculations do not validate a real fuel's condensed-product behavior. Heat capacities are for frozen composition, not equilibrium composition changes during expansion.

The built-in fuel compositions and formation enthalpies remain inherited assumptions. The existing inlet convention is preserved: scalar feed amounts use the reactant's minimum tabulated temperature. For the bundled live tables, N2O therefore enters as **gas at 200 K**, and the synthetic fuel at **298.15 K**. This is not a model of liquid nitrous injection enthalpy and is not inferred from the tank state. Direct solver calls can supply `(mass, temperature)` within each reactant's data range. Fuel single-temperature data do not support arbitrary inlet temperatures.

Agreement with Cantera verifies the numerical solution for these shared assumptions and data. It does not independently validate the data or predict agreement with a motor firing.

## Equations and sources

For each species, the logarithm of its mole fraction is the sum of element potentials weighted by its atoms, minus its standard Gibbs energy divided by RT, minus `log(P / P_ref)`. The solve enforces that fractions sum to one, each element's amount matches the feed, and total product enthalpy matches inlet enthalpy. Log-sum-exp evaluates the composition without overflowing. The reported error fields expose the final balances.

Thermodynamic basis: [NASA RP-1311, Part I](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19950013764/downloads/19950013764.pdf). Independent comparison: [Cantera equilibrium and NASA9 interfaces](https://cantera.org/3.2/python/thermo.html), with [explicit atomic weights](https://cantera.org/3.2/yaml/elements.html).
